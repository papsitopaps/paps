# Leoparding coach and hydration advisor

Code: `web/leopard.js`. Tests: `tests/leopard.test.mjs`.
These are practical rules gathered from Neapolitan pizza practice (AVPN guidance, and the usual advice of pizzaioli and pizza-oven makers). They are not laboratory constants, so use the bake log to tune them to your own oven and flour.

## What makes leoparding

Leopard spots are small dark char marks on a pale, puffy rim (cornicione).
Tiny gas bubbles just under the skin blow up in the heat. Their thin tops char before the rest of the rim browns.
To get them you need all of the following:

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Mature dough (long, mostly cold ferment) | It fills the dough with many fine surface bubbles. Young dough gives a few big blisters and a pale rim. |
| Enough water | Steam inflates the rim and the bubble tips. Too much water gives large pale blisters. |
| Very hot, fast bake (430–485 °C, 60–100 s) | It chars the bubble tips before the whole rim colours. Cooler ovens brown evenly. |
| No sugar or malt, little or no oil | Sugars brown the whole surface (Maillard reaction and caramelisation), so the spots disappear. |
| Balls tempered to room temp | Cold balls inflate unevenly and stay pale. |
| Flour strong enough for the ferment length | Weak gluten can't hold the gas, so the rim collapses or tears. |
| Technique | Little bench flour, gentle opening that keeps gas in the rim, a hot floor, and lifting the pizza toward the dome to finish. |

## Score

Each factor is rated good (1), ok (0.5) or bad (0), and weighted:

| Factor | Weight | Good | OK |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydration | 15 | advisor range | ±3 pts |
| Fermentation time | 15 | 24–72 h | 12–96 h |
| Tempering after the fridge | 12 | 3–6 h | 2–8 h |
| Oven | 18 | 430–485 °C | 400–510 °C |
| Bake time | 8 | 60–100 s | 45–120 s |
| Sugar and oil | 10 | none | oil only |
| Flour strength | 12 | W ≥ 220 + 2·hours (and not much stronger) | 30 below to 130 above that |
| Pre-ferment (contemporary only) | 5 | poolish or biga | none |
| Salt | 3 | 2.5–3.2% | 2–3.6% |
| Room temperature | 2 | 18–23 °C | 15–26 °C |

The score is the weighted percentage. Grades:

| Score | Grade |
|---|---|
| ≥ 85 | Leopard-ready |
| ≥ 65 | Close |
| ≥ 45 | Needs work |
| < 45 | Unlikely to leopard |

## Hydration advisor

The baseline is the AVPN guideline for true Neapolitan pizza: about 1 L of water per 1.7–1.8 kg of flour (56–59%), baked in a wood oven at about 485 °C for 60–90 s.

| Oven | Base hydration |
|---|---|
| ≥ 450 °C | 60% |
| 400–449 °C | 63% |
| 330–399 °C | 66% |
| home oven | 69% |

Hotter ovens bake faster, so the dough loses less water and needs less.

Adjustments:

- **Contemporary style:** +8 pts.
- **Warm kitchen:** −0.5 pt per °C above 22 °C (at most −3).
- **Cool kitchen:** +0.5 pt per °C below 18 °C (at most +2).
- **Flour strength:** −3 below W 240, −1 for W 240–279, +2 above W 330.
- **Long ferment:** −1 over 48 h.

The recommended range is the target ±2 pts.
